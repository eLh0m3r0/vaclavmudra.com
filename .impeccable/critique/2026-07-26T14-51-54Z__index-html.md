## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored, not generated — but the character is concentrated in the wrong section.**

**LLM assessment.** The hero schematic is the most specific thing on the page: seven ArchiMate-dialect nodes with registration crosses, elbow joins that turn at right angles, a travelling cobalt pulse per edge, one node acid-filled for the LLM. It *demonstrates* the service instead of naming it, and no unrelated product could reuse it unchanged. "Modrotisk" is load-bearing rather than decorative — it produces the depth rule (offset overprint, never blur), which is why the About photograph's misregistered cobalt frame reads as printed rather than as a shadowed card. The copy voice is unmistakably a person, and "Statické HTML. Žádné trackery." is a true statement about the page, which is the most confident move on the site.

Against that, three choices are category-interchangeable. The **sticky project deck** — full-viewport cards stacking, oversized name, metadata in the corners — is a 2022–2025 studio-portfolio default; swap "Assoluto" for any product name and the card is literally unchanged. The **counter-running marquee bands** are a skills cloud with better typography, and they are the only claim on the page with nothing behind them: "PostgreSQL ✳ MSSQL ✳ ArchiMate" is cashable against nothing, which Product Principle 1 forbids. The mono `01 —` eyebrows, the fixed circular scroll-to-top and fade-and-rise on every block are house style now.

The sharpest miss: the positioning is "running things anyone can open — not claims on a slide," and the deck shows no evidence anything runs. No screenshot, no production date, no artefact — a name at 84px, two lines of prose, and the URL rendered as the smallest, quietest element on the card. The hero shows its work; the deck abandons the instinct on the one section the entire argument depends on. Second miss: "three worlds at once, simultaneously" is encoded only as two acid squares four rows apart in a reverse-chronological list that reads as a sequence, not concurrency.

**Deterministic scan.** CLI detector on the live markup: **exit 0, 2 findings** — `em-dash-overuse` on `index.html` and `en/index.html` (13 em-dashes each, advisory, category `slop`). ## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely well-made site whose best instinct fires once and then gets abandoned. The hero proves a claim by drawing it; the About block proves discretion by practising it. Then the project deck — the section carrying the entire "shipped products as proof" positioning — presents five near-identical screens at 40–50% void where the live URL is the smallest, dimmest element on the card, and the $1.99 joke tool gets the same 84px treatment and the same `● LIVE` chip as the real SME product, at position 3 of 5 in the credibility sequence.

The single biggest opportunity is not visual. It is that the deck should show something only a shipped product can produce, and the contact block should tell the visitor what happens after they write. The page peaks in About and then spends its last screen looking decisive while giving the visitor nothing to act on.

## What's Working

1. **The hero schematic earns its space, which almost no hero graphic does.** Orthogonal elbow joins with a mid-height break, 6px registration crosses on two corners of every node, a travelling pulse per edge, one node acid-filled. A prospect who has seen an ArchiMate model recognises the dialect in under a second; one who hasn't still reads "this person draws systems." It also does the job a stock illustration would have wasted — in canvas, with zero external requests, so it costs the footer's "no trackers" claim nothing.

2. **The About block converts restraint into an argument.** Grayscale at `grayscale(1) contrast(1.05)` so cobalt stays the only chromatic event; the caption is an acid chip *inside* the print rather than a `Fig. 1` line beneath it. Beside it, a timeline naming the firms he contracted *through* and never the clients he delivered *to*. A prospective client infers their own name would be handled the same way — Product Principle 3 executed through layout instead of copy, which is the only way that claim is credible.

3. **Rules instead of cards, and typographic rather than boxy states.** Per-section grids (hero `1.15fr 0.85fr`, register `72px 1.1fr 1.3fr` baseline-aligned, sectors split by a vertical hairline) give structure with no bordered box anywhere. The register's hover is entirely typographic: title translates 14px, a cobalt `✳` fades in at `left:-1em`, the row number turns cobalt, and the row's box does not change. This is why the page reads as printed rather than as a component library.

Also worth crediting: the reduced-motion path is properly built and could not be broken. `styles.css:853–864` plus `main.js:226–238` yield a genuinely complete static page — reveals forced visible, timeline at `scaleY(1)`, canvas drawn with `elapsed = 99999`, bands and glyphs untransformed.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The project deck proves nothing — on the one section the whole positioning depends on

**What.** Each card is a name at 84px Archivo, two lines of prose, two tags, and the live URL as the smallest, dimmest element on the card (12px mono, bottom-right). `margin-top: auto` pushes the description down, leaving 40–50% of every card void — worst on Geodaily, where a one-line description leaves ~250px of nothing. No screenshot, no production date, no artefact. DissConnect (a $1.99 joke) carries the same visual weight and the same `● LIVE` chip as Docházka.net.

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md's core claim is that a neighbouring consultant can describe the same competencies but almost none can hand over live URLs to products they built and operate. The design renders that hand-over as *metadata*. The prospect who came to verify "he ships" leaves with the information a slide would have given — and with the joke tool at position 3 of 5 in the proof sequence.

**Fix.** Invert the card hierarchy: make the live URL a primary, unmistakably clickable object at 24–32px rather than 12px foot metadata. Fill the dead ~250px with something only a shipped product can produce — a real screenshot in the same grayscale-plus-cobalt register as the About photo, or a factual line ("v provozu od 2023", "open source, N commits"). Separate the joke from the production products: lighter treatment, or out of the numbered sequence.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

### [P1] No reassurance at the moment of decision

**What.** The contact block offers an address, LinkedIn, GitHub, "Praha, CZ" — and nothing else. No response expectation, no engagement shape, no prompt for what to write, no restated availability. `mailto:` carries no `?subject=`, so the visitor lands in a blank compose window.

**Why it matters.** This is the entire conversion event. The primary persona is an SME owner who buys IT rarely and reluctantly and wants to know what happens *after* he writes *before* he writes. PRODUCT.md excludes testimonials, a "Hire me" CTA and Calendly — none of which forbids one dry line of scope or response expectation. The page's confidence peaks in About and then goes silent exactly where cost-of-action needs lowering.

**Fix.** One or two factual sentences above the address, in the established dry voice: what a first message usefully contains, and a real response window. Add `?subject=` to the `mailto:`. Restate availability here in readable type instead of leaving it 8,000px back on a rotating textPath.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The availability stamp destroys the schematic's SaaS node at every width

**What.** Measured overlap of the stamp's bounding box against the SaaS node: **95% at 1440px**, 43% at 1200, 19% at 1000, 26% at 920, 33% at 760, 45% at 600, 46% at 480 and 390. At 1440px — the most common desktop width — one of the seven nodes is entirely obliterated, the "SaaS" label mangled between the box's top edge and the stamp's outer ring. Both objects are cobalt, so they merge into noise rather than reading as deliberate overlap.

**Why it matters.** Two things break at once. The schematic is the page's most specific asset and it ships visibly damaged — and because DESIGN.md's misregistration language is deliberate *only* on the About photo, here it reads as a bug, which a visitor cannot distinguish from one. Meanwhile the availability signal, the one piece of information that makes writing feel worthwhile, is rendered as illegible rotating circular text on top of a diagram, in the place a visitor is least ready to act.

**Fix.** Move the SaaS node or the stamp so they never intersect at any width — the canvas positions are normalized constants at `main.js:66–74`, so this is a coordinate change plus a stamp anchor that respects the node's box. Then reconsider whether the stamp belongs in the hero at all, rather than beside the contact heading where its sentence is actually needed.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] Mobile is a 9,124px document with one nav link, and the fixed button covers the language switch

**What.** At 390px, `Co dělám`, `Pro koho`, `Projekty` and `O mně` are all `display:none` — only `Kontakt` plus the CS/EN chips remain, across a 9,124px page (10.8 screens) with no section indicator. The CS/EN chips measure **33×26px, 4px apart** — under the 44×44 minimum and adjacent. The role chip breaks: at ≤390px "Solution Architect" splits across two lines with the acid highlight fragmenting into two ragged blocks (at ≤320px both chips split) — that is iPhone 12/13/14/15 width, and the role string is a stated Brand Commitment. At the bottom, the fixed 48px topstar covers **58% of the footer's "English" link at 390px and 60% at 1000px**.

**Why it matters.** The visitor arrives mid-evaluation, often on a phone. Dropping navigation means the proof section the positioning depends on is reachable only by scroll-hunting. The only control pinned in the thumb zone takes the user *away* from the CTA. And the language switch — on a site whose fourth product principle is that both languages are first-class — is physically obscured at two common widths.

**Fix.** Keep `Projekty` and `Kontakt` at all widths. Give the CS/EN chips 44×44 hit areas. Add `white-space: nowrap` to `.hero__role .hl`, or stack the two roles below 430px. Reserve bottom/right padding in `.footer` for the fixed button, or hide the topstar once the footer intersects.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Load-bearing information is set at 3.35:1, and the two mutations have drifted

**What.** `--ink-3` (`#7f8985`, **3.35:1** on cool white) at 11–12px carries the work-history dates (`.tl__period`), degree and certification years (`.about-meta dt`), the "PŘES" label, the deck's only interaction hint (`.section-head__note`) and the inactive language chip. Three independent sources agree: the design review flagged it, the overlay detector flagged it, and the recomputation confirmed 3.35:1 against a required 4.5:1. DESIGN.md already states this token is "decorative-only until it is darkened", and PRODUCT.md commits to secondary text ≥4.5:1 — so the shipped page contradicts its own recorded standard. Separately, confirmed parity drift: **EN sector chips lack the `✳`** and read "Private sector · SME" against CS's "✳ Soukromý sektor · SME".

**Why it matters.** The verifier persona's entire job is reading dates against a CV, and the dates are the least legible text on the page. The inactive `EN` chip at 3.35:1 / 11px is the only language affordance in the header — an integrator-side contact has to hunt for it.

**Fix.** Promote `.tl__period`, `.about-meta dt`, `.section-head__note`, `.tl__via .label` and the inactive lang chip to `--ink-2` (7.16:1), or darken `--ink-3` until it clears 4.5:1. Add the `✳` to both EN sector chips.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

## Minor Observations

- **1.52 MiB of dead assets.** `assets/prod-meme.png` (924K) has zero references anywhere — the markup reference was removed in `98e3172` and the file was left behind. `assets/photo-dog.jpeg` (636K) is referenced only by `/old/`. The `unicode-range` split buys nothing on the CS route, because Czech copy pulls every `latin-ext` subset anyway; it just doubles the request count per family. A Czech-first single subset per family would cut requests without losing a glyph.
- `data-email-copied` exists on `en/index.html:391` and not on `index.html:391`. **Not a live bug** — `main.js:333` falls back to a hardcoded Czech string, so CS happens to render correctly. It is latent fragility: the Czech copy lives in the JS instead of the markup.
- Section-head gloss appears on 01 and 03 only (confirmed: 2 occurrences per file). Commit to five or drop it.
- Two timeline entries are marked current (Aricoma 10/2024 and freelance 06/2014), four rows apart in a list ordered by start date. The concurrency PRODUCT.md calls the differentiator reads as an ordering mistake.
- The timeline's "current" marker is fill-colour only, while chips elsewhere encode status three ways. Inconsistent with the page's own stated redundancy rule.
- `.index-list__num` is `aria-hidden` decoration occupying a fixed 48px column at 390px — 12% of mobile measure spent on a number nobody reads, while the description beside it wraps to five lines.
- Genesis Factory's URL is 38 characters of 12px mono at 0.6 alpha on ink — the longest and dimmest link on the page.
- The footer's only two links are "Archiv v4" and "English". One of them serves nobody in the audience, and it comes first.
- `sizeSchematic` only re-rasterises when `canvas.width !== w*dpr` (`main.js:84`), so a height-only viewport change — mobile browser chrome collapsing on scroll — leaves the canvas at the previous aspect ratio.
- No `og:image:alt`, and `og:description` differs from `meta description`. The social card is the first impression for referral traffic and is not being treated as a designed surface.
- Any automated consumer that doesn't scroll sees a near-empty page: all 22 `[data-reveal]` blocks are `opacity: 0` at `scrollY: 0`.

## Questions to Consider

- The hero draws a system diagram to prove he architects systems. Why does the deck, which exists to prove he *ships*, show nothing that has shipped?
- The footer says "Statické HTML. Žádné trackery." and it is true. What else on this page could be *true* rather than claimed — a commit date, a build size, a project's uptime, a status read from something real?
- "Otevřený ke spolupráci" is set on a rotating textPath 8,000px from the contact block. If that sentence is worth saying, why is it the least readable text on the page and absent from the place where it decides the outcome?
- The marquee bands are the only claim on the page with nothing behind them. Delete them — does a prospect lose anything, or does the page lose the thing that most makes it look like every other one-pager?
- Five cards, five register rows, five timeline entries, five nav items, "Pět věcí." Is five a finding or a habit? PRODUCT.md says the deck is variable-length — what does this layout look like with three projects, or eight?
- A visitor decides to write. What does the page want them to type? Right now the answer is nothing, and `mailto:` hands them a blank window.
- On mobile, the only thing pinned to the screen takes you *away* from the CTA. If exactly one control could be fixed in the thumb zone, why isn't it the email?
- Everything is invisible until scrolled into view. What would the confident version look like — content simply *there*, with motion reserved for the two or three moments that earned it?
